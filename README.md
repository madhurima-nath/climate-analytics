# Climate Intelligence: Using Climate Data to Improve Business Decision-Making
### University of Reading | FutureLearn

---

## Course Overview

I took this course to learn more about how climate affects business decision-making and to get a clearer picture of what climate data actually looks like in practice. Climate as a subject was not new to me, but I wanted structured grounding in how it translates into data, decisions, and business risk — and to understand what kinds of roles and credentials exist in this space.

The course covers two weeks of structured learning. Week 1 focuses on how different types of businesses use climate and weather data in practice, through three case study interviews. Week 2 covers the science and mechanics of climate data: where it comes from, how models work, what types of predictability exist, and how to interpret model output responsibly.

---

## Key Learning Outcomes

The main takeaway reinforced something I already suspected: climate is genuinely complex, and solutions for one problem do not transfer to others. The three case studies make this concrete. EnergyMetric uses 10 years of historical reanalysis data and Type 0 predictability because at a 10-20 year energy planning horizon, natural variability matters more than the long-term climate signal. Historic England uses long-term Type II projections because heritage assets are irreplaceable and the planning horizon is decades to centuries. Airspace Unlimited uses 72-hour Type I weather forecasts because their entire operation runs on knowing what the wind is doing now. Same broad topic — climate and weather — three entirely different data needs.

The framework for thinking about predictability (Type 0, I, and II) and the distinction between epistemic and aleatory uncertainty are the most transferable concepts from this course. They apply to any data problem, not just climate.

The next step is to move from understanding the concepts to working with actual climate data — building a project that demonstrates what these scenarios look like in practice, how complicated the choices are, and what the outputs mean. That project will complete this portfolio.
